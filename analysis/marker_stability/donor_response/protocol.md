# Do donor-labelled groups differ reproducibly in their responses to individual drugs?

This supplementary analysis follows the primary marker-state experiment. Its autosomal aggregate results and the donor counts by organ were already known. The donor-labelled contrasts had not been computed when this specification was written. The question is whether the magnitude of a repeatable male-minus-female difference in drug responses exceeds what arbitrary line groups of the same sizes would produce.

Use the frozen 948-gene autosomal panel and the original repeat plates. Start from the 24 lines eligible for the marker analysis. Keep known donor labels and organs with at least two lines of each donor sex. That gives eight bowel lines (4 female, 4 male) and eleven lung lines (3 female, 8 male). Skin has one eligible female line. Within each organ keep only drug–dose conditions with at least 200 cells in every selected line on each plate, and the same vehicle-depth rule used by the marker study. Compare the identical drug and dose between plates. Retain every qualifying gene and condition.

For each line–plate, transform drug and pooled vehicle RNA counts to log₂(1 + CPM) and subtract vehicle. Subtract each line and gene's mean response over the eligible drugs on that plate. This centers the question on group differences that depend on which drug was given and removes group differences that are constant over drugs. For each organ, drug and gene take the mean centered response in male-donor lines minus the mean in female-donor lines on each plate. Multiply the two plate differences and average across all genes and drug conditions. Effects with consistent direction contribute positively, irrespective of which group has the higher expression. Report the two organ values and their unweighted mean.

A positive cross-product can arise for arbitrary groups because the same lines are present on both plates. Compare the true value with every whole-line permutation of donor labels within organ, retaining group sizes. The directional hypothesis is greater repeatable separation than arbitrary groups. Report the fraction of permutations at least as large as observed (including the observed assignment), the mean of the permutation distribution, and observed minus that mean. Also enumerate the same comparison restricting permutations within organ and binary Y/XIST marker group. Do not select drugs, genes, cutoffs, or interpretations by their individual responses. Positions repeat across plates, related lines may remain dependent, and labels are observational. These data cannot identify a causal sex effect, toxicity in patients or an effect of any particular drug.
