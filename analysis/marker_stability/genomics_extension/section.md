# Do lines with the same Y/XIST marker status have similar drug responses?

## Methods

We compared treatment-induced expression changes in two repeat plates of the Tahoe-100M cancer cell atlas. Both plates contained 94 matching drug–dose conditions and two vehicle wells. We selected the 948 autosomal genes among the 978 directly measured landmarks defined by the Connectivity Map L1000 assay. Ensembl gene IDs identified 30 X-linked landmarks, which we excluded so that sex-chromosome genes did not enter both the grouping and the response. Within each plate, well and cell line, we summed RNA counts across all captured cells. For each gene we subtracted log₂(1 + counts per million) in the two pooled vehicle wells from the corresponding drug measurement. Total transcript counts across all genes supplied each library denominator. [Tahoe-100M](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.035) · [L1000 landmarks](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2017.10.049)

The primary panel comprised bowel, lung and skin lines from the preceding recovery analysis. We grouped them by their baseline RNA markers: expression above threshold for either Y markers or XIST, or for neither. For a given drug–dose, both lines had to contribute at least 200 cells to each treated well and to each of the four vehicle wells. For each distinct pair of lines from the same organ, we correlated their 948-gene response vectors across plates, exchanged the plate assignments, and averaged the two correlations. We then averaged across eligible drug–dose conditions for each pair. Within an organ we gave the two same-status groups equal weight and subtracted the mean for opposite-status pairs. We weighted the three organs equally. The exact two-sided test reassigned whole-line labels within each organ while preserving group sizes. Its interpretation assumes that the marker labels are exchangeable between lines within an organ. The repeat plates shared drug positions. [Tahoe plate design analysis, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01604)

## Results

Twenty-four of 26 prespecified lines met the vehicle threshold. They contributed 2,218 paired line–condition measurements, 93 eligible drug–dose conditions and 97 distinct within-organ pairs. Each pair shared 89–93 eligible conditions. Across the 24 lines, the mean correlation for the same line exposed to the same drug on the second plate was 0.385. The corresponding mean for the same line exposed to other drugs was 0.201. The matched-drug correlation was higher for every line. Because each drug occupied the same position on the two plates, this difference includes any reproducible position effect.

For different lines in the same organ, sharing marker status increased response similarity by an estimated 0.0109 correlation units (exact two-sided permutation P = 0.204, 249,480 assignments). The bowel estimate was +0.0006 across nine lines and the lung estimate was −0.0012 across eleven. The four skin lines had the largest estimate (+0.0334), but each marker group contained only two lines. Omitting skin gave −0.0003. Requiring identical drug coverage across all lines within an organ gave +0.0110. The split-vehicle control gave −0.0067. These data provide little evidence that this pooled Y/XIST marker status identifies lines with generally similar autosomal drug responses. A drug-specific effect, or different responses among Y-positive and XIST-positive lines, remains possible.

As an assay control, we examined four established glucocorticoid-responsive transcripts after treatment with 5 µM triamcinolone. Across the 43 eligible lines, all four genes had positive median changes on both plates. FKBP5 showed the largest median change, +2.99 and +1.64 log₂(CPM + 1) units on plates 6 and 14. DDIT4 changed by +0.87 and +0.31, PER1 by +1.46 and +0.82, and SGK1 by +0.14 on each plate. These medians summarize RNA from surviving, captured cells. Neither these measures nor the baseline RNA labels establish chromosome copy number, drug sensitivity or patient response. [Glucocorticoid-responsive transcripts across tissues](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-025-11676-w)
